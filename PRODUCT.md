# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Working software developers, mostly in the .NET and Azure ecosystem, plus architects and tech leads weighing patterns and tooling decisions. They land here from Google, LinkedIn, or a shared link while solving a real problem: how to structure a modular monolith, how to wire up Aspire, whether an approach is worth adopting. They read on a laptop between tasks or on a phone during a commute. They want the answer and the reasoning behind it, not a wall of preamble.

A second audience is people evaluating Daniel himself: prospective consulting clients, conference organisers, and folks curious about the side projects. They arrive at the About or Projects page and decide in seconds whether this person knows their craft.

## Product Purpose

Dan Does Code is Daniel Mackay's personal blog and home base. It exists to:

- Establish authority in .NET and software architecture through deep, practical writing that engineers trust and cite.
- Grow reach, so posts get shared, rank well, and pull readers into the newsletter and LinkedIn following.
- Showcase side projects (Subby, Gym Buddy, Dollarbucks, the Claude Code statusline, and more) and get people actually trying them.

Success looks like a developer finishing a post, feeling like they learned something they can use today, and coming back the next time they hit a related problem. The writing is the product; everything else stays out of its way.

## Brand Personality

Warm and approachable. A senior engineer explaining something to a peer over coffee, not lecturing from a stage. Confident about the material without being smug, generous with context, happy to admit trade-offs and the messy middle. Three words: **approachable, credible, practical.**

The tone is first-person and honest. Real code, real output, real "here's what tripped me up." It should feel like a person wrote it, because one did.

## Anti-references

- **Cluttered, ad-heavy reading experiences** (Medium-style newsletter walls, popups, dense sidebars, engagement bait). Nothing interrupts the read.
- **Try-hard flashy** (excessive animation, neon-on-black, gimmicks). Motion and colour serve comprehension or they don't ship.
- **Generic SaaS-starter sameness.** The site should feel like Daniel's, not the default template it started from.

## Design Principles

1. **The writing comes first.** Every layout decision protects the reading experience. If a flourish competes with the prose, the prose wins.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Real output over screenshots of internals, concrete examples over abstract claims. Practise what the posts preach.
3. **Warm expertise.** Credible and senior, but never cold. Approachability is a feature, not a compromise of authority.
4. **Calm by default, accent with intent.** Sky blue and pink are anchors used sparingly. Restraint is what makes the accents land.
5. **Respect the reader's time and attention.** No dark patterns, no walls, no bait. Fast, legible, and out of the way.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** for all body text, **AAA** where the palette already allows it (most primary text pairings clear AAA).
- Full light and dark theme support, respecting the system preference.
- Body-text links use the AA-safe strong blue (`#0284C7` light / `#38BDF8` dark); the brighter sky and the pink accent are reserved for large text, headings, inline code, and decorative use.
- Never rely on colour alone; pair with weight, size, or icons. Visible focus rings on all interactive elements.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; motion is an enhancement, never required to understand content.
